Clock Synchronization

My ongoing research focuses on nanosecond-level clock synchronization for large-scale networks and its applications, achieved using swarm-based consensus mechanisms. Rather than relying on traditional top-down architectures like NTP or PTP, network nodes repeatedly probe peer swarms to process timestamp measurements, estimating clock offset and drift to converge on a unified time. External reference signals (such as GPS or atomic clocks) propagate through the swarm, maintaining tight global alignment while mitigating local jitter and path asymmetry errors.

  • Firefly: Scalable, Ultra-Accurate Clock Synchronization for Datacenters

Software Defined Mobile and Testbed Networks

During my PhD and post-doctoral work at Stanford University, I worked alongside key figures in modern networking, making foundational contributions to early Software-Defined Networking (SDN) architectures. My research focused on extending SDN principles and virtualization to campus, home, and mobile wireless networks through key projects like OpenFlow, FlowVisor, and OpenRoads. A major milestone of this work was developing seamless mobility management, enabling mobile devices to transition smoothly between different Wi-Fi access points and cellular/WiMAX networks without dropping active TCP connections or streaming sessions.

Wireless Sensor Networks

My early research focused heavily on wireless sensor networks (WSNs), with a particular emphasis on body sensor networks (BSNs) and human-centric sensor systems. This work explored network architectures and link-layer behaviors at 2.4 GHz for body area networks to support continuous, energy-efficient, and non-intrusive physiological monitoring of patients. This included investigating trade-offs between star and multi-hop architectures to optimize power consumption across energy-constrained on-body sensors. Additionally, he spearheaded projects like MAX, a human-centric physical search system that used sensor tags to help users dynamically locate physical objects while addressing privacy, location tracking, and efficient search mechanisms.

  • Max: human-centric search of the physical world
  • Simple Directional Antennas: Improving Performance in Wireless Multihop Networks.
